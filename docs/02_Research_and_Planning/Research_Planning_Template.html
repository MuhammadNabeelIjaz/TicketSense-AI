RULES

How to use this

This is a template. Every findings box is empty on purpose: fill it with what you find, and cite where you found it.

Work through the 13 research areas in sequence. Each area takes two pages: the first tells you what to research and why, the second is your record of findings, sources, decisions and open questions. The readiness gate at the end has two checkpoints: one before you train anything, one before you build the app.

1
Read the area
2
Investigate
3
Record
4
Decide
Rules before you start
01
Follow the sequence. Each area lists what it needs and what it unlocks. Do not jump to models before the task, data and evaluation areas are done.
02
Write in your own words and attach a source to every factual claim. Copying text from a source is not research.
03
Tag every finding as Confirmed, Reported or Assumption (see the recording guide). Decisions should rest on Confirmed findings.
04
A decision needs a reason and a source or test. If it is only a preference, write “preference”.
05
Leave unknowns blank or write “not found” with where you looked. Never fill a gap with a plausible guess; move it to Open questions.
Each area follows the same pattern
Page 1 · Guidance (pre-written)
Research area · Purpose · Research questions · What to investigate · Expected findings
Page 2 · Your record (empty)
My research findings · Sources and evidence · Decisions and conclusions · Open questions
Minimum Path: how much to answer
Answer only the marked questions (a red dot, 49 of 93), in one to three lines each. MIN PATH areas need those answers; LIGHT areas (2, 3, 8) need a short read; Area 11 is OPTIONAL. The unmarked questions are the Full Track. This template holds findings and decisions only. Whether an area is finished is recorded in the Tracker (Doc 3).

How to record research

Every area uses the same structure, so your notes stay comparable.

The nine fields in every area
#FieldWritten byWhat goes in it
01Research areaPre-filledThe topic, with the area it depends on.
02PurposePre-filledWhy it matters. Use it to judge when you have done enough.
03Research questionsPre-filledAnswer each by number. Add your own.
04What to investigatePre-filledStarting points, not a complete list.
05Expected findingsPre-filledThe kind of information to collect; use it as a completeness check.
06My research findingsYouYour words, each point tagged Confirmed, Reported or Assumption.
07Sources and evidenceYouTitle, URL, type, rating and a one-line takeaway.
08Decisions and conclusionsYouOnly after research: the decision, the reason, your confidence.
09Open questionsYouWhat is unresolved, and how you will find out.
Evidence labels
Confirmed
You read the primary source or ran the test yourself.
Reported
A secondary source says so; you did not verify it.
Assumption
Plausible but unchecked. Do not base a decision on it.
Source rating
A
Primary: official docs, dataset card, licence text, peer-reviewed paper, your own experiment.
B
Secondary with method: engineering post, preprint, reputable tutorial.
C
Unverified: marketing, forum posts, summaries without references. Never decide on C alone.
Illustrative example · not a real finding
Finding (Confirmed, 5-2): [what the dataset page says about its licence, in your words] Source 5-2: [title] · [URL] · type: dataset card · rating: A · takeaway: [one line] Decision: [use / do not use / use with conditions] because [reason] (5-2) · confidence: [high / medium / low] Open question: [what is still unchecked?] -> how I will find out: [action]
Cite as area-row. Source 5-2 means area 5, row 2 of its sources table. Record the date you accessed each web source.

07Evaluation design7 questions
MIN PATHNeedsArea 4Area 6Draws onArea 3UnlocksArea 8Area 9Area 10Area 11Area 13
Purpose

Decide how you will judge any model before you compare models. This protects you from fooling yourself with a lucky split, a leaked test set or a flattering metric.

Research questions

Which metrics suit this task, why, and which one is primary?
7.2
How will you split data into training, validation and test sets? How do you stratify, and how do you keep near-duplicates apart?
7.3
When is cross-validation appropriate, and for what purpose?
7.4
How will you read a confusion matrix and analyse errors systematically?
7.5
Where could leakage enter your pipeline, and how will you check each place?
7.6
How will you build the short, misspelled, ambiguous and out-of-category tests the brief asks for?
7.7
How will you report results honestly given synthetic data, small test sets and run-to-run variation?
What to investigate
01
Documentation of the metrics you plan to use and how averaging across classes works.
02
Evaluating imbalanced classification: what accuracy hides.
03
Test-set discipline: when it may be used and how many times.
04
Error-analysis practice: how to group and count mistakes.
05
Basics of variation across random seeds and what it means for your claims.

01
A written evaluation protocol: metrics, primary metric, splits, seeds, rules.
02
A stress-test plan (next page).
03
An error-analysis template.
04
A reporting checklist and the limitation statements you already know you will need.

Evidence on whether heavy cleaning helps or hurts linear text models.

10Confidence, uncertainty and human review7 questions
MIN PATHNeedsArea 7Area 9Draws onArea 3UnlocksArea 12
Purpose

Work out what a model's confidence really means, how to decide when a person should check a result, and how human corrections could be recorded and used.

Research questions
10.1
What does a class probability mean for each candidate model, and why is it not a guarantee of correctness?
10.2
How can you test whether confidence is trustworthy?
10.3
How can a “needs human review” threshold be chosen, and what trade-off does it create?
10.4
How do real systems treat uncertain predictions: suggest, preselect, leave empty, route to a default queue?
10.5
What should the user see: all class probabilities, the top few, a flag?
10.6
How will corrections be recorded (fields, format, privacy), and what would retraining on them require?
10.7
What can go wrong in a feedback loop: biased corrections, drifting labels, reinforced errors?
What to investigate
01
Probability calibration and calibration curves in the library documentation.
02
Selective classification: models that abstain when unsure.
03
Human-in-the-loop and active-learning basics.
04
What your area 3 sources say about thresholds and corrections.
05
Concept drift: how a model ages after deployment.
Expected findings
01
A plain definition of “confidence” in your system.
02
A method for choosing the review threshold, and the trade-off it creates.
03
A draft record format for corrections.
04
A retraining policy, even if the answer is “manual and later”.
05
A list of feedback-loop risks.
